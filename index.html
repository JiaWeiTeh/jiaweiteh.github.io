About

I am a PhD candidate at the Institut für Theoretische Astrophysik, Universität Heidelberg, within the International Max Planck Research School for Astronomy and Cosmic Physics, supervised by Ralf Klessen, Simon Glover and Kathryn Kreckel.

I study stellar feedback and the evolution of interstellar bubbles in giant molecular clouds. I am the lead developer of TRINITY, a one-dimensional thin-shell feedback code, and apply it to resolved surveys of nearby galaxies and Galactic star-forming regions (PHANGS, SDSS-V Local Volume Mapper, JWST). I am a member of the PHANGS and SDSS-V/LVM collaborations and the ECOGAL ERC Synergy consortium.

Before Heidelberg I studied astronomy and astrophysics at the Australian National University.

Research

Feedback-driven bubbles in molecular clouds

Molecular clouds in nearby galaxies are cleared within a few million years of forming their first massive stars, before any of those stars explode as supernovae. TRINITY follows the pre-supernova feedback channels together — stellar winds, direct and dust-reprocessed radiation pressure, and the pressure of photoionised gas — by integrating the equation of motion of a single swept-up shell from the first winds to the shell's fate: dispersal of the cloud, or stalling and re-collapse. It succeeds WARPFIELD, keeping that code's speed while adding photoionised-gas pressure as an explicit driver and a choice of cloud density profile. Paper I presents the model and its validation; I am now using it to survey cloud mass, density, star-formation efficiency and metallicity.

Confronting the model with resolved observations

The model predicts quantities that can be compared directly with data: the shell's size and velocity, the balance of forces driving it, and the fraction of ionising photons that escape. I am using SDSS-V Local Volume Mapper integral-field spectroscopy of the Rosette Nebula to test the feedback history of its central cluster, and I contribute TRINITY models to studies of the young bubble in the central starburst of NGC 253 with JWST and of neutral atomic hydrogen around the Orion nebula.

Escape of ionising photons from H II regions

My earlier work combined LEGUS star-cluster catalogues with SIGNALS H II-region spectroscopy of NGC 628 to constrain the fraction of Lyman-continuum photons that escape individual H II regions, a thread continued in the PHANGS study of the stellar associations powering H II regions.

Software

TRINITY — a one-dimensional thin-shell code for the feedback-driven expansion of bubbles in molecular clouds. Given a cloud's mass, density profile, star-formation efficiency and surroundings, it integrates the shell's radius, velocity, thermal state and force budget through its phase transitions to its stopping fate. Pure Python, GPL v3. Source code · Documentation
