I am interested in the galactic baryon cycle, focusing on how young stars disperse their natal molecular cloudsThe Orion A molecular cloud in the infrared. Credit: ESO/VISION survey (resized) and redistribute gas into the surrounding interstellar medium. To do so, I develop TRINITY, a code that models the evolution of interstellar bubbles under the influence of important stellar feedback processes (stellar winds, photoionised gas and radiation). I also work with observations from the PHANGS and SDSS‑V Local Volume Mapper surveys to test these models against real star‑forming regions.

About me

I am a PhD candidate at the Institut für Theoretische Astrophysik, Heidelberg University, where I have worked with Ralf Klessen, Simon Glover and Kathryn Kreckel since 2022. I am also part of the International Max Planck Research School for Astronomy and Cosmic Physics, and a member of the PHANGS and SDSS-V/LVM collaborations and the ECOGAL ERC Synergy consortium.

Before my PhD, I studied astronomy and astrophysics at the Australian National University (Canberra, Australia), and graduated with first-class honours in 2021. As an undergraduate, I did summer research projects at CSIRO, Leiden Observatory and Swinburne University of Technology.

Outside astronomy, I like hiking, taking photos, bouldering and playing the piano. I also enjoy picking random towns in Germany and cycling there.
